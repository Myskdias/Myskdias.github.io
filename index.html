01 / Research

Research

Current research2026

Shandess

Towards a general-purpose model of learner histories

I currently develop language-model-based predictors of student behavior conditioned on a learner’s interaction history. My long-term goal is to build a model that can answer natural-language questions about a learner based on their past interactions.

Read more about Shandess

The project initially focused on Knowledge Tracing: predicting whether a learner will succeed or fail on a given exercise. These predictions could ultimately support personalized learning trajectories for large numbers of students. This intended use places several constraints on the model: it must provide accurate predictions, remain fast and inexpensive to run, and generalize to learners and exercises that were not observed during training.

Meeting these requirements raised a central architectural challenge: how can a small language model with a limited context window use a long and information-rich interaction history? I studied this problem along two dimensions: how to encode and compress the history, and how to inject the resulting representation into the language model. I developed a dedicated History Encoder and compared different conditioning mechanisms, including soft prompting and gated cross-attention. This work resulted in Shandess, a compact Knowledge Tracing model that outperformed the evaluated baselines while remaining suitable for local inference.

Separating the representation of the history from the prediction task opened a broader research direction. I extended the project to Answer Prediction, where the model predicts which answer a learner will select, and investigated whether a single model could handle both tasks through natural-language prompts. Alternating between Knowledge Tracing and Answer Prediction during supervised training did not produce a satisfactory unified model. I am now using RLOO to train a reasoning model and investigate whether reasoning over the interaction history can enable more flexible behavior across different questions.

A research paper on Shandess is in preparation.

Supervised by Pierre-Yves Oudeyer and Loris Gaven at Inria Flowers.
